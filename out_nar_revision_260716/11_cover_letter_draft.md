Dear [Editor name / Editorial Board],

Re: Revised submission of "[Manuscript title]" (Ms. ref. [NAR-XXXXX])

We thank the reviewers for their careful and constructive assessment. The central concern —
that the manuscript presented several results at equal weight and thereby *read as straining
for statistical significance* — was well taken, and it has guided the revision. Rather than add
analyses, we have **sharpened what the study claims and demoted what it merely reports**. The
result is a paper organised around a single, defensible message, with phenomenon-level and
inference-limited material moved to the Supplement and its supporting statistics retained in the
main text. We summarise the principal changes below; a full point-by-point response follows.

## 1. One central message, clearly foregrounded
We have restructured the Abstract and Introduction around a single organising finding: **the two
methylation systems are spatially organised relative to the regulatory genome and do not instruct
transcription.** The 4mC reassignment and the same-strand dual 4mC/6mA modification, previously
presented as co-equal "principal findings", are now framed as enabling and independent technical
observations that set up — rather than compete with — the organising result. We deliberately use a
relational rather than an agentive verb ("spatially organised relative to", not "organises"),
because our observational time course cannot establish the causal direction of the
methylation–transcription relationship, and we did not wish to over-claim it.

## 2. Selecting only what the data and prior work support
We have moved the study's most inference-limited result — the temporal compartment co-dynamics
(Spearman ρ = 0.43, effective n ≈ 3) — out of the Abstract, retaining in that location only the
robust static co-localisation of methylation with the active chromosomal compartment (odds ratio =
1.96, p = 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁷). The ρ = 0.43 result remains in the Discussion with its existing
low-degrees-of-freedom caveat. We have added a short scope paragraph stating explicitly what the
study does and does not claim, so that our negative results are read as pre-specified tests, not
as failed positives.

## 3. Phenomenon-reporting data moved to the Supplement
Following the reviewers, the negative-control comparison (former Figure 2d), the 6mA
redistribution companion, and the merged growth/antibiotic-production panel have been relocated to
the Supplement. Their load-bearing statistics remain in the main text: in particular, the
permissive result — that Exposed and Shielded regulators are equally likely to change expression
(44% vs 44%; Cliff's δ = −0.010, 95% CI [−0.16, +0.15]; TOST rejecting a >±0.5 log₂FC difference,
p = 0.01) — is retained as a designed falsification test that establishes the permissive model.

## 4. A substantive figure correction
Re-examination of the promoter protection zone (Reviewer comment on Figure 2b) identified that the
shaded "protection zone" band had been drawn over a ~2,200 bp interval that is, in the all-gene TSS
metagene, essentially at baseline (99% of the distal median). The genuine depletion is confined to
~±300 bp of the transcription start site (down to 77% of baseline; minimum at −100 bp). We have
redrawn the band to ±300 bp and corrected the corresponding Introduction sentence. The ~2,200 bp
figure belongs to the separate regulatory-gene symmetric-zone analysis and has been re-attributed
accordingly.

## 5. Figure and notation corrections
All specific figure points have been addressed: core/arm boundary now stated on Figure 1a and the
*oriC* marker recoloured to neutral grey; overlapping significance markers in Figure 1c re-spaced;
Figure 1d motif-logo titles set to black; Supplementary Figure 10 notation unified to "4mC"
throughout, the detached text box replaced by an integrated summary panel, and the AAGCCCG 6mA site
count corrected to the canonical value (441; 22.80% occupancy).

## 6. New comprehensive survey of prokaryotic epigenetics
In response to the request for a comprehensive assessment of prior prokaryotic-epigenetics work, we
have surveyed seven areas of the field and, against it, delineated which of our claims are novel,
which are bounded limitations of the present study, and which lie outside its scope.

We believe these changes address the reviewers' concerns in full and yield a substantially clearer
and more disciplined manuscript. We would be glad to provide any further clarification.

## Two points on which we invite the editor's / reviewers' guidance
- One reviewer referred to a dataset we were unable to identify from the comment as transcribed
  ("Mutilka"); the pathway-enrichment analysis in Figure 7 is built entirely from the present
  study's own methylation-proximal gene sets (n = 3,197 / 459 / 448) tested against the *S.
  coelicolor* M145 KEGG annotation, and does not draw on an external dataset. We would welcome
  clarification if a specific external resource was intended.
- Orthogonal chemical confirmation of the 4mC assignment and identification of the responsible
  methyltransferases remain outside the scope of this sequencing-only study; both are stated as
  bounded limitations, and the 4mC assignment is phrased throughout as a base-modification-calling
  result supported by internal specificity controls.
