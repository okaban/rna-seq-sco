# Abstract

Bacterial DNA methylation is widely assumed to regulate transcription, yet genome-wide evidence for this function beyond a few model systems remains limited. Here we integrate Oxford Nanopore methylome and RNA-seq data across three developmental stages of *Streptomyces coelicolor* A3(2) strain M145 to systematically test whether two active methylation systems — GCCGGC (N4-methylcytosine) and AAGCCCG (N6-methyladenine) — function as transcriptional regulators.

We show that apparent genome-wide methylation-expression correlations are spurious associations driven by geographic confounding from the linear chromosome's core/arm functional differentiation: all three significant associations identified in unstratified analyses vanish upon geographic stratification (e.g., GCCGGC de-repression: *p* = 8.3 x 10^-8 unstratified, *p* = 0.87 core-only). Instead, we describe a four-layer methylation-exclusion architecture. The restriction-modification systems undergo complete geographic redistribution (T1-T2 Jaccard = 0.000) without transcriptional consequences. A ~2,200 bp methylation-free protection zone at regulatory gene promoters, created by evolutionary counter-selection of recognition motifs and collective protein occupancy at promoters, co-occurs with the promoters of 94.6% of regulatory genes (998/1,055). The remaining 57 exposed regulators (5.4%), defined by a 293 bp distance boundary (AUC = 0.917), are structurally co-localized with a distributed transcriptional transition pattern at the vegetative-to-developmental boundary. These 57 genes are organized into two antagonistic co-expression blocs — an activation bloc (TCS, sigma factors, RamR) and a repression bloc (TetR-enriched, *p* = 0.028) — whose upregulation and downregulation occur simultaneously at the exponential-to-transition boundary. Notably, the magnitude of methylation change and the magnitude of expression change are uncorrelated within these 57 genes (T1-T2 *rho* = 0.136, *p* = 0.299; T2-T3 *rho* = 0.012, *p* = 0.927), arguing against a direct causal driver role for methylation dynamics.

No evidence was found that DNA methylation directly controls transcription in *S. coelicolor*. Rather, a structural dichotomy at regulatory gene promoters spatially co-localizes methylation accessibility with a specific minority of regulators that accompany a coordinated developmental transition, revealing a previously unrecognized organizational principle of bacterial regulatory genomes.
