# STREME Motif Discovery on Unassigned 6mA Sites — Results Paragraph

To test whether the 1,497 6mA sites at T1 that fell outside the canonical AAGCCCG window (±3 bp) harbour an additional, undescribed recognition sequence, we performed de novo motif discovery with STREME (MEME Suite v5.5.9; `--minw 6 --maxw 12 --nmotifs 5`) on 91-bp windows centred on each unassigned site. STREME returned five motifs with E-values ranging from 3.0×10⁻³ to 9.1×10⁻² (Supplementary Table S_STREME). Motif 2 (CAAGCCCGCCV, E = 1.4×10⁻²; 118 sites) is a residual AAGCCCG hit whose sharply off-centred site distribution (peaks at positions 41–49 of the 91-bp window) reflects assignment-window leakage rather than a distinct recognition sequence. The only candidate without an obvious explanation, Motif 1 (CGGCGACGRCGA, E = 3.0×10⁻³; 353 sites), has no REBASE match and shows a flat positional distribution across the window, in contrast to the tightly centred peaks expected for a methyltransferase target; its E-value is also several orders of magnitude weaker than that of the canonical AAGCCCG motif recovered in the parent 6mA set (E ≈ 10⁻²² in the 4mC-residual control; Supplementary Table S_STREME). The remaining motifs (CTGCTCGCC, CGCCCYCRCC, CCSGGTTSCYG) are short, GC-rich, low-complexity sequences consistent with the 72% GC background of the *S. coelicolor* genome. Together, these results provide no high-confidence evidence for a novel 6mA recognition sequence among the unassigned sites, and we interpret the unassigned fraction as a combination of stochastic methylation, low-IPD ambiguous calls, and assignment-window leakage. A complete list of motifs, site counts, and E-values for both the 6mA unassigned and 4mC residual STREME runs is provided in Supplementary Table S_STREME.
