Materials and Methods

Bacterial strains and growth conditions
S. coelicolor M145 strain (specimen voucher: ATCC BAA-471) and mutant strains were grown
on solid soy flour-mannitol (SFM) medium (20 g/L mannitol, 33 g/L soya flour, 20 g/L agar) at
28ºC for sporulation. Then the spores stocked in 20% glycerol were inoculated to 2×TY medium
(16 g/L tryptone, 10 g/L yeast extract, 5 g/L NaCl) and germinating in rotary incubator with 30ºC
and 220 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 8 h. The total cells were collected and inoculated to
liquid R5- medium containing stainless steel coil and grow about 14 h to M phase (OD450 = 0.5 ~
0.7) or 22 h to L phase (OD450 = 1.5 ~ 2.0) in rotary incubator with 30ºC and 220 rpm.
Composition of the liquid R5- medium is as following: 25 mM TES (pH 7.2), 103 g/L
sucrose, 10 g/L glucose, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10.12 g/L MgCl2·6H2O, 0.25 g/L K2SO4, 0.1 g/L
casamino acids, 0.08 mg/L ZnCl2, 0.4 mg/L FeCl3·6H2O, 0.02 mg/L CuCl2·2H2O,                 0.02
mg/L MnCl2·4H2O,      0.02   mg/L Na2B4O7·10H2O         and   0.02   mg/L   (NH4)6Mo7O24·4H2O
and adjusted pH to 6.8 with 5 M NaOH. The total cells of different samples and growth phase
were harvested and proceeded to further investigation. E. coli DH10B was used as the
cloning host and E. coli ET12567/pUZ8002 was used for intergeneric conjugation. All E. coli
strains were cultured in 2×TY medium at 37ºC.

The construction of plasmid and Streptomyces mutant
S. coelicolor strains, E. coli strains, plasmids and designed sgRNA have been summarized in
Tables S2–5. DNA manipulations were performed by standard protocols (1).
    For plasmids construction, different sgRNA targeted to each HCR-MN and HCR-LN
windows were one-step clone to NheI and XbaI digested Streptomyces code-optimized spCas9
born plasmid pWHU2653 with promoter kasO*p and B1006 terminator by Gibson assembly.
Then a series of left and right homologous arms together with the gus(a) reporter cassette or
the RK-682 recombinant BGC cassette were introduced to the HindIII site of these sgRNA born
plasmids by Gibson assembly to achieve plasmids pGAM1 ~ 10, pGAL1 ~ 10, pRKM1 ~ 10
and pRKL1 ~ 10, respectively. The gus(a) reporter cassette or RK-682 recombinant BGC
cassette was integrated into PvuI and XbaI linearized pSET152 to produce pGAattB or pRKattB,
respectively.
    For S. coelicolor mutants construction, each plasmid with pWHU2653 backbone was
transformed from E. coli ET12567/pUZ8002 to S. coelicolor by intergeneric conjugation with
standard protocols (2). The apramycin-resistant exconjugants were patched on SFM plate with
25 μg/mL apramycin and grown at 28ºC for 2 or 3 days to confirm their antibiotic resistance
and then transferred to a SFM plate without antibiotics using plate streaking. The single clones
were then patched to SFM plates with 25 µg/mL apramycin and SFM plates without antibiotic,
respectively. To screen for the recombinant mutant, the candidate mutants with apramycin-
sensitive phenotype were further verified by PCR and sequencing. Two plasmids with
pSET152 backbone were transferred to S. coelicolor by intergeneric conjugation and the
apramycin-resistant exconjugants were also verified by PCR and sequencing.

Measurement of glucuronidase activity

                                                2
For the glucuronidase activity test, S. coelicolor wild-type and mutant strains were cultured in
liquid R5- medium as previous description. After reaching the specific growth phase, the whole
cells were harvested by centrifugation and resuspended with 20 mL 40% glycerol. 15 mL of the
cell suspension was separated and centrifugated, then washed twice with distilled water. This
part was then evaporated to dryness under reduced pressure and then measure the dry
weight. The rest 5 mL cell suspension was also centrifugated, the supernatant discarded and
then resuspended with 10 mL GUS buffer (50 mM phosphate buffer, pH 7.0; 5 mM DTT; 0.1%
Triton X-100). The cell suspension was then lysed by sonication. 200 µL cell lysate with six
repeats was transferred to 96-well plates and then supplemented with 5 µL 100 mM p-
nitrophenyl-β-D-glucuronide. Incubating at 37°C, the optical density at 415 nm was measured
per 5 min for a total of 40 minutes. Glucuronidase activity (U/g) in our case was calculated as:
ΔOD415 (0 min to 40 min)/dry weight (g).

Isolation, detection, and quantification of RK-682
For RK-682 production and analysis, S. coelicolor wild-type and mutant strains were cultured
in liquid R5- medium as the previous description. After reaching the specific growth phase, the
whole cells were harvested by centrifugation and then washed twice with distilled water. These
cells were evaporated to dryness under reduced pressure and then measure the dry weight.
These dry cells were extracted with 40 mL ethyl acetate and the extracts were then evaporated
to dryness and redissolved in 1 mL methanol for further analysis. Analysis of RK-682 was
performed with a Phenomenex C18 column (5 µm, 4.6 × 250 mm) with elution at a flow rate of
1 mL/min over a 22 min gradient as follows: time T = 0, 85% B; T = 14 min, 95% B; T = 18 min,
isocratic gradient with 95% B; T = 19 min, 85% B; T = 22 min, isocratic gradient with 85% B
(phase A, 20 mM ammonium acetate; phase B, methanol). LC-ESI-HRMS analysis was carried
out on an LTQ XL Orbitrap (Thermo Fisher Scientific) coupled with an Accela photodiode array
(PDA) detector, Accela PDA autosampler, and Accela pump, using electrospray ionization in
the negative-ion mode. The selective ion monitoring was carried out on [M+H]- (m/z, 367.2479)
ions of RK-682 and the area of extracted peaks was counted. Relative RK-682 production
(U/mg) in our case was calculated as: peaks-area/dry weight (mg).

Statistical information
The Microsoft Excel software package and Graphpad Prism software were used for statistical
calculations and data visualization. All data presented are means ± standard errors calculated
from three independent experiments.

RNA extraction
Total RNA was extracted from the tissue using TRIzol® Reagent according to the manufacturer’s
instructions (Invitrogen) and genomic DNA was removed using DNase I (TaKara). Then RNA
quality was determined by 2100 Bioanalyser (Agilent) and quantified using the ND-2000
(NanoDrop Technologies). Only high-quality RNA sample (OD260/OD280 = 1.8~2.0, OD260/OD230
≥ 2.0, RIN ≥ 6.5, 28S : 18S ≥ 1.0, concentration ≥ 100 ng/μL, mass ≥ 2 μg) was used to
construct sequencing library.

Library construction and sequencing

                                               3
RNA-seq transcriptome library was prepared following TruSeqTM RNA sample preparation Kit
from Illumina (San Diego) using 2 μg of total RNA. Shortly, ribosomal RNA (rRNA) depletion
instead of poly(A) purification is performed by Ribo-Zero Magnetic Kit (Epicenter) and then all
mRNAs were broken into short (200 nt) fragments by adding fragmentation buffer firstly.
Secondly double-stranded cDNA was synthesized using a SuperScript double-stranded cDNA
synthesis Kit (Invitrogen) with random hexamer primers (Illumina). When the second strand
cDNA was synthesized, dUTP was incorporated in place of dTTP. Then the synthesized cDNA
was subjected to end-repair, phosphorylation, and ‘A’ base addition according to Illumina’s
library construction protocol. The second strand cDNA with dUTP was recognized and
degraded by the UNG enzyme. Libraries were size selected for cDNA target fragments of 200
bp on 2% Low Range Ultra Agarose followed by PCR amplified using Phusion DNA polymerase
(NEB) for 15 PCR cycles. After quantified by TBS380, the paired-end RNA-seq sequencing
library was sequenced with the Illumina HiSeq × TEN (2 × 150 bp read length). The processing
of original images to sequences, base-calling, and quality value calculations were performed
using the Illumina GA Pipeline (version 1.6), in which 150 bp paired-end reads were obtained.
A Perl program was written to select clean reads by removing low-quality sequences, reads
with more than 5% of N bases (unknown bases) and reads containing adaptor sequences.

RNA-seq analysis
htqc (3) (version 0.90.8) was used to filter the sequencing data to make clean data. Then the
cleaned reads were mapped onto the S. coelicolor genome (NC_003888.3) using SOAP (4)
(2.2.1). The significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified by edgeR (5) with
definition of log2 fold change (log2FC) >1 or < -1 and false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05.

Hi-C library construction
Hi-C libraries were constructed according to previous studies (6). Briefly, bacterial cells were
cross-linked with 3% formaldehyde for 1 hour at 4°C and quenched with 375 mM final
concentration glycine for 15 min. The cross-linked cells were incubated with lysozyme at 37°C
for 10 min to lyse the cells. Endogenous nucleases were inactivated with 0.5% SDS, then
chromatin DNAs were digested by 50 U Sau3AI (NEB) and marked with biotin-14-dCTP
(Invitrogen) and then ligated by 50 U T4 DNA ligase (NEB). After reversing cross-links, the
ligated DNA was extracted using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen) according to the
manufacturers’ instructions. Purified DNA was sheared to 300 ~ 500 bp fragments, then blunt-
end repaired, A-tailed and adaptor-added before purification through biotin-streptavidin–
mediated pull-down and PCR amplification. Finally, the Hi-C libraries were quantified and
sequenced on the Illumina Nova-seq platform (San Diego).

Construction of contact map
After quality filtering using htqc (3) (version 0.90.8), the clean Hi-C data from two biological
replicates for M phase and L phase, was iteratively mapped to the S. coelicolor genome
(NC_003888.3) using the hiclib (7) software package (version 1f8815d0cc9e). Dandling ends
and other unusable data were filtered, the valid pairs were used to analyze the correlation
efficiency of the two biological replicates for each sample using QuASAR-Rep analysis (8)
(3DChromatin_ReplicateQC v 0.0.1), then we pooled the data from the two replicates together

                                               4
for further analysis. The valid pairs after pooling were binned into 40 kb, 5 kb and 2 kb
nonoverlapping genomic intervals to generate contact maps. The contact maps were
normalized using an iterative normalization method to eliminate systematic biases.

Compartment A/B analysis
The CscoreTool (v1.1) (9) software was applied to identify A and B compartments on 40 kb
resolution. Genomic bins with a positive or negative c-score signa were divided into the A or B
compartment. The active “A” compartments are active gene-enriched regions, whereas the
inactive “B” compartments are inactive gene-enriched regions.

Chromosomal interaction domains (CIDs) analysis
CIDs are contiguous regions that display high levels of self-association and that are separated
from adjacent regions by distinct boundaries. The locations of CIDs were determined at 5 kb
resolution using Tung B. K. Le’s methods (10). For each bin, interactions between that bin and
bins upstream or downstream within 200 kb were extracted to form two vectors. T-values of
paired test of Log2 values of the two vectors were calculated to form a directional index (DI). A
CID boundary is determined as the DI value upstream a bin is below -1.81 and downstream
above 1.81 (T-value at which P-value = 0.05, degree of freedom = 10).

Calculation of significant interactions
The contacts between 5 kb bins of interactions of each sample were transferred to Ay’s Fit-Hi-
C software (v2.0.0) (11) (with parameter settings-L 5000 - p 2) to calculate the corresponding
cumulative probability P-value and Q-value. After calculation, the interactions in which both the
P-value and Q-value were less than 0.01 and the contact count > 2 were identified as significant
interactions.

Calculation of the FIRE value
The FIRE value was calculated referring to Schmitt A. D.'s methods (12). First, the genome
effective fragment length and GC represent content and mappability (F_GC_MAP) were
computed using the F_GC_MAP.file.sh script from the HiCnv software (GitHub - ay-lab/HiCnv:
HiCnv is used to call copy number variations and breakpoints from Hi-C data). Second, the
local interactions (contact sum of distance range of 15 ~ 200 kb for each bin from 5 kb raw
matrix) were counted. Third, the HiCNormCis algorithm is the same as HiCNorm (13). They are
both Poisson-regression-based normalization but HiCNorm is work on 2D data while
HiCNormCis takes on 1D data. We modified the HiCNorm software code to take HiCNormCis
calculation using the local interactions and F_GC_MAP of each bin. The code file was
appended as SI dataset 5.

Comparison of FIRE-like signals with RNA-seq
FIRE-like signals were calculated using Schmitt A. D.'s methods (12). RNA-seq coverage
signals and FIRE-like signals were z-transformed and Gaussian Smoothed using Virgina S’
methods (2) to measure the correlation between transcription and local contact.

Construction of the chromosomal 3D structure


                                               5
Pastis (v0.1) (14) was used to construct the chromosomal 3D structures using a 5 kb contact
map with a multidimensional scaling (MDS) model.

Data availability
The genome sequence of S. coelicolor A3(2) was obtained from NCBI (Reference Sequence
ID: NC_003888.3). The RNA-seq and Hi-C seq data generated during this study have been
deposited in the NCBI BioProject under the accession code: PRJNA891940.

kinds of cassette were preferred to integrate between two operons with the same transcriptional

sgRNAs were designed for different target integration of gus(a) report cassette (Table S4). (C)
Integrative plasmid target to attB site with the same gus(a) report cassette as B. (D)
Heterologous expression plasmid with entire RK-682 BGC under the control of two individual
endogenous promoters (pactII-orf4). (E) CRISPR/Cas9 based plasmids comprising fused entire
RK-682 BGC under the control of one constitutive promoter ermEp1*, sandwiched by two
bidirectional fd-phage terminators. A series of homologous arms and sgRNAs were designed
